# Is the best model biased towards surface measures? The best set without them

Generated by `curated_features/modeling/run_surface_ablation.py`. The cognitive features (sentence length, and the old parse features that include tokens per sentence) carry more than half of the best model's importance, and the labels follow word and sentence length closely. Here they are removed step by step. Gradient boosting; test = the 865 real-Finnish test texts.

## Summary

- The best set misses by 0.257 (5.1%) on the test set (trained on real-Finnish rows). Without all cognitive features: 0.382 (7.6%); also without the length-like vocab features: 0.425 (8.5%).
- On `FI` alone (labels vary, the source cannot explain them), trained on FI rows: 0.277 (5.5%) for the best set, 0.409 (8.2%) without the cognitive features, 0.395 (7.9%) without the length-like vocab features too; a constant guess gives 1.287 (25.7%) and the 4 surface measures 0.398 (8.0%).

## How to read the numbers

- **Average miss (MAE)** with a 95% interval from resampling the texts; smaller is better. One label step is 0.5. A percent in brackets after an average miss is that miss divided by the label range (5 points, labels 1 to 6).
- **FI only** is graded on the 310 FI test texts after training on the FI train rows only (729 texts): the one source whose labels vary, so recognising the source does not help.
- **The 4 surface measures** (the reference row): number of words, number of characters, mean word length and mean sentence length.
- **Length-like vocab features**: `avg_word_length`, `long_word_ratio`, `n_unique_lemmas` (grows with text length) and `n_compound_tokens` (a count).
