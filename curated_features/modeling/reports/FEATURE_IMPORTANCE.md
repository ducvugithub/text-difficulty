# What the best model relies on: permutation importance by feature group

Generated by `curated_features/modeling/run_feature_importance.py`. Model: vocab + grammar_coverage + cognitive (new), 311 features, gradient boosting, trained on the real-Finnish train texts; the test set (865 texts) has an average miss of 0.257 (5.1%).

## Summary

- The group that matters most is **cognitive: sentence length**: shuffling its 4 features raises the average miss by 0.571.
- The single most important feature is `cognitive_sent_mean_len`: mean sentence length in words (shuffling it raises the miss by 0.517).
- 44 of 311 features have an importance above 0.002; the rest add almost nothing on their own.

## How to read it

- **Permutation importance**: the values of a feature (or of a whole group of features) are scrambled, i.e. exchanged between the test texts, and we measure how much the performance gets worse. A group is scrambled as a block: all its features move together, so a text keeps the relationships among the group's own features.
- **Importance** = how much the average miss (MAE) grows after the scramble. Bigger means the model relies on it more. One label step is 0.5. A percent in brackets after an average miss is that miss divided by the label range (5 points, labels 1 to 6).
- **Spread** = how much the importance varies over 5 different random scrambles; small means the number is stable.
- **Direction** = the rank correlation of the feature with the label on the training texts: **harder** means a higher value goes with a harder text, **easier** the opposite.
- Features that carry the same information share their importance, so a single feature can look small even if its topic matters. The group table avoids this.
- This shows what the model uses, not what causes difficulty. In this data the labels follow word and sentence length closely, so those measures rank high.
